# Occupational Health, Symptom Triage & Generic Medicines Guide for Gig Workers

- **Issuing Body:** FairWork Setu Occupational Health & Clinical Safety Council
- **References:** National Health Mission (NHM), National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP)
- **Emergency Helplines:** 108 (Ambulance), 112 (National Emergency), 102 (Maternal/Child Emergency), 14555 (Ayushman PM-JAY), 1800-180-8080 (Jan Aushadhi Helpline)
- **Target Population:** Platform and gig delivery riders, auto/cab drivers, domestic workers, street vendors, and logistics workers across India.

---

## 1. Respiratory Infections: Cold, Cough, Sore Throat & Nasal Congestion (जुकाम / सर्दी / खांसी / कफ)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Jukam, zukaam, zukham, sardi, thandi lag gayi, nazla, khansi, khasi, sukhi khansi, balgam wali khansi, kaf, cough, gala kharab, gale me dard, gale me kharash, chheenk, naak behna, naak band, saans lene me sarsarhat, sinus, thand lag gayi, bike chalane se sardi.
- **Immediate Shift First-Aid & Self-Care:**
  1. **Warm Hydration:** Sip lukewarm water or herbal/ginger-tulsi tea during work shifts. Keep a thermos of warm water on your bike. Avoid cold sodas, chilled water, or abrupt shifts between AC restaurants and scorching outdoor roads.
  2. **Steam Inhalation:** Inhale steam from plain boiling water for 10 minutes at night and after finishing shifts to loosen nasal and bronchial mucus.
  3. **Warm Salt-Water Gargle:** Gargle with 1 glass warm water mixed with half teaspoon salt twice daily to relieve throat soreness and reduce viral inflammation.
- **Subsidized Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) Generic Medicines:**
  - *Paracetamol 500mg / 650mg:* ₹5 to ₹10 per strip of 10 tablets (relieves fever, headache, and body aches).
  - *Cetirizine 10mg:* ₹5 to ₹8 per strip of 10 tablets (relieves allergic sneezing, runny nose; take 1 tablet at night before sleep).
  - *Dextromethorphan / Ambroxol cough syrup (100ml):* ₹25 to ₹35 at PMBJP stores (versus ₹110 to ₹140 for commercial branded syrups).
- **⚠️ Danger Signs (Refer to UPHC / Civil Hospital):**
  - Persistent fever exceeding 101°F for more than 3 consecutive days.
  - Coughing up yellow-green purulent sputum or traces of blood.
  - Shortness of breath, rapid wheezing, or chest tightness.

## 2. Dehydration, Heat Stress & Sunstroke (निर्जलीकरण / लू / डिहाइड्रेशन / अत्यधिक प्यास)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Dehydration, dihydration, lu lagna, loo lag gayi, dhup lag gayi, dhoop me chakkar, garmi chadh gayi, pyaas, gala sookh raha hai, hoth sookh rahe hain, paseena nahi aa raha, bahut paseena beh gaya, sar ghoom raha hai, kamzori lag rahi hai, behoshi jaisa lag raha hai, chakkar aana, exhaustion, heat stress, heat cramps.
- **Immediate Shift Protocol:**
  1. **Stop & Park in Shade:** Pull over your bike or vehicle immediately into shade. Turn off ignition.
  2. **Cool Down:** Remove helmet, jacket, and tight delivery backpacks/vests. Sponge forehead, nape of the neck, and wrists with a cool damp cloth.
  3. **Hydrate Slowly:** Sip Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) solution slowly. Drink 1 glass over 10-15 minutes.
  - **Warning:** Do NOT gulp down ice-chilled water or refrigerator drinks rapidly; sudden temperature shock triggers severe stomach cramps, vomiting, and vascular constriction.
- **Subsidized Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) Electrolytes:**
  - *Jan Aushadhi WHO-formula ORS Sachet (21.8g):* ₹4 to ₹5 per sachet (versus ₹22 to ₹30 for commercial branded Electral).
  - Fresh coconut water, thin buttermilk (chaas) with rock salt, or lemon-water with sugar and a pinch of salt serve as natural alternatives.
- **🚨 Critical Red-Flag Emergency (Heat Stroke - Call 108):**
  - High core body temperature (>103°F) accompanied by the *complete absence of sweating* (hot dry skin).
  - Mental confusion, slurred speech, delirium, disorientation, loss of consciousness, or repeated severe vomiting.
  - Call 108 immediately and pour room-temperature water continuously over the worker's head and chest until the ambulance arrives.

## 3. Fever, Viral Infection & Body Ache (बुखार / बदन दर्द / वायरल / हरारत)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Bukhar, bukhaar, buhar, fever, hararat, tap, taap, shareer garam hai, badan dard, badan toot raha hai, ang ang toot raha hai, sir dard, sar phat raha hai, thakan, susti, kapkapi, thand lagkar bukhar, viral, dengue jaisa lag raha, malaria, chills.
- **Immediate First-Aid & Rest Protocol:**
  1. **Strict Rest:** Stop strenuous outdoor riding; physical exertion during fever increases myocardial strain and accelerates dehydration.
  2. **Tepid Sponging:** Sponge forehead, arms, and legs with normal room-temperature water. Do NOT use ice water.
  3. **Fluids:** Drink at least 2.5 to 3 liters of fluids daily (lukewarm water, thin dal, ORS, vegetable soup).
- **Subsidized Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) Guidance:**
  - *Generic Paracetamol 500mg:* 1 tablet every 6 to 8 hours after food (maximum 2,000mg/day for gig workers without liver disease). Strip of 10 costs ₹5 to ₹10.
  - **CRITICAL CLINICAL SAFETY WARNING:** Do NOT consume Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Combiflam, or unprescribed NSAIDs without medical examination. If the fever is Dengue, these drugs cause platelet inhibition and life-threatening internal gastrointestinal hemorrhage!
- **⚠️ UPHC Referral Indicators:**
  - Fever accompanied by severe retro-orbital eye pain, joint/bone pain, red petechial skin rashes (Dengue warning).
  - Chills with teeth-chattering rigors recurring every alternate day (Malaria warning).
  - Dark Coca-Cola colored urine, extreme fatigue, or yellowing of the eyes (Jaundice / Hepatitis warning).
  - Get a free CBC, Dengue NS1 antigen, and Malaria smear at your local government Urban Primary Health Centre (UPHC).

## 4. Digestive Upset, Acidity, Gastritis & Food Poisoning (पेट दर्द / गैस / एसिडिटी / उल्टी-दस्त)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Pet dard, pet me marod, stomach pain, acidity, gas ban rahi hai, seene me jalan, khatti dakar, ulti, vomiting, ji machal raha hai, matli, dast, loose motion, dast lag gaye, pet kharab hai, hazma kharab, food poisoning, bahar ka khana khane se pet kharab.
- **Immediate First-Aid & Dietary Steps:**
  1. **Prevent Dehydration:** Consume 1 cup of fresh ORS solution after every loose motion or vomit episode.
  2. **Bland BRAT Diet:** Eat freshly prepared curd rice, plain moong dal khichdi, bananas, and toast. Avoid roadside cutting chai, fried samosas, spicy curries, and unwashed raw street salads.
  3. **Rest the Gut:** Avoid carbonated beverages and sugary energy drinks which worsen osmotic diarrhea.
- **Subsidized Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) Generic Medicines:**
  - *Jan Aushadhi ORS Sachet:* ₹4 to ₹5 per sachet.
  - *Generic Pantoprazole 40mg or Omeprazole 20mg:* ₹10 to ₹18 per strip of 10 tablets (take 1 tablet on an empty stomach 30 minutes before breakfast for acid reflux and burning chest sensation).
  - *Antacid Oral Suspension (200ml):* ₹25 to ₹35 at Jan Aushadhi stores (relieves acute heartburn).
  - *Dicyclomine 10mg / Mefenamic:* ₹8 to ₹12 per strip under pharmacist guidance for acute abdominal spasms.
- **⚠️ Danger Signs (Seek Immediate Medical Care):**
  - Bloody or black tarry stools.
  - Inability to keep any liquid down for more than 6 hours with severe dry tongue and sunken eyes.
  - High fever (>102°F) with intense localized lower-right abdominal pain (acute appendicitis red-flag).

## 5. Musculoskeletal Strain, Backache & Rider Ergonomics (कमर दर्द / गर्दन का खिंचाव / जोड़ दर्द)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Kamar dard, back pain, peeth dard, gardan akdna, neck stiffness, kandha dard, shoulder pain, ghutna dard, haath pair dard, nerves khinch gayi, bike chalane se kamar dard, delivery bag uthane se dard, moch aa gayi, muscle cramp, nas chad gayi.
- **Rider Ergonomics & Shift Protocols:**
  1. **Mount Bag on Cargo Rack:** Never carry heavy 10-15 kg delivery backpacks exclusively on your shoulders for 10-hour shifts. Fasten the backpack to the bike rear carrier using sturdy bungee cords.
  2. **Active Decompression Breaks:** Take a 3-5 minute posture break every 2 hours: roll shoulders backwards, do gentle neck stretches, and arch the lower back while standing.
  3. **Adjust Handlebars & Seat:** Ensure bike handles allow elbows to stay slightly bent; locked rigid elbows transfer road shock directly into cervical vertebrae.
- **At-Home Relief & Jan Aushadhi Remedies:**
  - Apply warm compress (hot water bottle) for 15-20 minutes on the lower back or neck after returning home.
  - *Jan Aushadhi Diclofenac Gel / Pain Spray (30g):* ₹20 to ₹25 (versus ₹120 to ₹160 for commercial branded spray like Volini).
  - *Generic Paracetamol 500mg:* ₹5 to ₹10 for acute muscular aching. Avoid long-term self-medication with strong painkiller pills to protect renal health.

## 6. Minor Wounds, Burns & Silencer Injuries (मामूली चोट / खरोंच / साइलेंसर बर्न)
- **Synonyms & Dialect Terms:** Chot lag gayi, khronch, cut lag gaya, silencer se jal gaya, bike silencer burn, chhil gaya, ungli kat gayi, halka khoon aaya, minor injury, burn dressing, road scrape.
- **Bike Exhaust Silencer Burn Protocol:**
  1. **Immediate Cooling:** Hold the affected burned skin under gentle, clean, room-temperature running tap water for 10 to 15 minutes continuously.
  2. **Do NOT:** Never apply toothpaste, ice cubes, engine oil, butter, or raw turmeric onto fresh burns—these trap heat inside tissues and cause severe microbial infections.
  3. **Do NOT Pop Blisters:** Intact skin blisters (fafola) serve as sterile biological band-aids protecting underlying dermis.
  4. **Antiseptic Burn Ointment:** Gently apply *Silver Sulfadiazine 1% cream* (Jan Aushadhi: ₹15 to ₹20 per 20g tube; commercial brands: ₹80 to ₹120) and cover loosely with sterile paraffin gauze.
- **Road Scrapes & Abrasions:**
  - Wash road dust and grit gently with soap and clean tap water.
  - Apply *Povidone Iodine 5% ointment* (Jan Aushadhi: ₹12 to ₹18 per 15g tube) and protect with a sterile gauze bandage.
- **⚠️ Tetanus Toxoid (TT) Injection Requirement:**
  - If your skin was cut or punctured on a road surface, rusty metal, or dirty pavement, and you have not received a Tetanus Toxoid (TT) booster within the last 6 months, visit the nearest government dispensary or UPHC within 24 hours. A government TT shot costs only ₹10.

## 8. Critical Emergency Red-Flag Protocol (Direct Bypass - Immediate 108 / 112)
- **Life-Threatening Emergency Warning Signs:**
  1. Crushing chest pain, severe tightness radiating to the left arm, neck, or jaw, accompanied by profuse cold sweat (acute myocardial infarction / heart attack).
  2. Severe shortness of breath, gasping for air, blue-tinted lips, or choking.
  3. Sudden loss of consciousness, fainting, or unresponsive collapse.
  4. Sudden facial droop, arm weakness, inability to speak or slurred speech (acute stroke warning).
  5. Uncontrolled arterial spurting or severe continuous bleeding following a road accident.
  6. Suspected poison ingestion, chemical splash, or snakebite.
- **Immediate Response Actions:**
  - **Dial 108:** Free 24x7 Government Emergency Ambulance with basic or advanced life support.
  - **Dial 112:** All-India Unified Emergency helpline for police, fire, and trauma coordination.
  - **Dial 102:** Free dedicated transport for pregnant women and neonates.
  - Proceed immediately to the nearest 24x7 Government Medical College, District Civil Hospital, or trauma casualty.
