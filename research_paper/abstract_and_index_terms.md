## Abstract

Over 500 million informal workers in the Global South, including 7.7 million gig platform laborers in India (projected to expand to 23.5 million by 2030), encounter severe socioeconomic precarity driven by extreme daily earning volatility, algorithmic management opacity, lack of structured accounting records, low functional literacy, and persistent exclusion from statutory social safety nets. This research presents **FairWork Setu**, an accessible, voice-first livelihood companion and financial machine learning architecture specifically designed for informal gig workers. We introduce a dual-layer artificial intelligence framework that integrates server-side specialized machine learning microservices with client-side mathematical edge heuristics to guarantee uninterrupted sub-5 millisecond responsiveness during mobile connectivity dropouts. 

Our core empirical contributions encompass:
1. **Autoregressive Cash-Flow Forecasting:** A 100-tree Random Forest regressor with 14 engineered temporal and trigonometric cyclical lags, achieving a 47.1% Mean Absolute Error (MAE) reduction (₹76.60 vs. ₹144.81 baseline, RMSE ₹123.25, $R^2 = 0.5594$) over standard rolling averages, paired with a 2-layer PyTorch Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) sequence model converging to an MSE of 0.00806 across 35 epochs;
2. **Code-Mixed Hinglish NLP:** A lightweight natural language processing pipeline coupling sublinear TF-IDF vectorization with Multinomial Logistic Regression to categorize colloquial, code-mixed Hinglish transaction narrations across 8 informal expense classes with 75.0% accuracy and a 0.736 macro F1-score;
3. **Outflow Anomaly Detection:** An unsupervised Isolation Forest anomaly detection model isolating irregular outflows (mean anomaly score -0.1657 at 0.05 contamination);
4. **Deterministic Information Extraction:** A deterministic finite-state natural language understanding automaton delivering 100.0% currency and credit/debit direction extraction; and
5. **Dense Semantic Retrieval:** Dense bi-encoder semantic embeddings (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) indexed via FAISS Flat-L2, achieving a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of 1.000 in semantic gig matching and an MRR at Rank 4 of 0.9167 across four statutory Indian welfare acts.

These empirical findings substantiate that domain-adapted, lightweight machine learning architectures deployed at the mobile edge deliver actionable, resilient financial intelligence without requiring computationally prohibitive cloud dependencies.
